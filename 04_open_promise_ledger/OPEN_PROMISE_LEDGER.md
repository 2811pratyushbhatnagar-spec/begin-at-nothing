# Open Promise Ledger v0.1

## A design proposal for consent-bounded promise-flow

Money, credit, reputation, governance, and coordination can be read as promise systems.

This document proposes a ledger design that records only the traces needed for coordination, accountability, and repair without reducing persons to scores.

## Core idea

A ledger should track promises, not souls.

It should make hidden cost visible without consuming private life.

It should help communities repair broken promise-flow without turning every relation into surveillance.

## Ledger primitives

### Person / boundary

A person is not a ledger entry.

The ledger may record consented traces about promises involving a person, but it must never claim to represent the whole person.

### Promise

A commitment, expectation, duty, exchange, or relation that must hold through time.

### Trace

A record that something happened, changed, failed, or was repaired.

### Visibility

Who can see what, for what purpose, and for how long.

### Cost

What was required to preserve continuity: money, labor, attention, care, ecology, privacy, trust, risk, or repair.

### Repair

The path when a promise fails, becomes unclear, harms someone, or exceeds scope.

## Design principles

1. Local-first.
2. Consent-bounded.
3. Minimal disclosure.
4. Trace without totalization.
5. Repair before punishment where possible.
6. No person becomes a score.
7. No global layer consumes private life.
8. No hidden cost.
9. No action without contestability.
10. Forkable and auditable protocols.

## Local money and credit

Local currencies, community credits, time-banks, mutual aid records, or contribution ledgers can be promise-flow instruments.

But they should not become coercive identity systems.

A healthy local ledger asks:

- What promise was made?
- What was delivered?
- What remains unresolved?
- Who or what carried hidden cost?
- What repair is possible?
- What privacy boundary applies?

## Governance

Governance begins where promise-flow affects multiple boundaries.

A dispute is not only a violation.

It is a signal that promise, boundary, trace, cost, or repair needs attention.

The ledger should help direct attention toward repair without making surveillance the default.

## AI role

AI can help summarize promise contexts, detect imbalance, flag hidden cost, suggest repair paths, translate disputes into clearer records, protect boundaries, and reduce administrative burden.

AI must not become judge, owner, state, or priest; score persons as whole beings; expose private life by default; optimize extraction; hide ecological or social cost; or act without promise, boundary, trace, cost, and repair.

## Public line

The Open Promise Ledger is not a social credit system.

It is a consent-bounded accounting layer for promises, costs, traces, and repair.

The person is never the ledger.
